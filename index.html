One day, eight stops.

From the old timber wharf in Woolloomooloo to the glowing sails at Vivid Sydney – ten and a half hours along the harbour, seamless, and almost entirely on foot or by ferry.

The Plan

The route stays right along the waterfront all day. It begins east of the city centre in Woolloomooloo, loops through Circular Quay featuring the museum and Opera House, takes an afternoon detour through the Royal Botanic Garden, and ends after dinner in the illuminated spectacle of Vivid Sydney.

Between stops, you'll rarely walk for more than twenty minutes. If you prefer a slower pace, skip the national park and spend more time in the gardens – the rest of the itinerary still works perfectly.

Woolloomooloo

Bay east of the city centre · Starting point

A narrow bay featuring a massive timber pier: Finger Wharf was built between 1911 and 1915 for wool processing and was long one of the world's largest wooden structures. Today it houses apartments, a hotel, and restaurants, though the historic timber framework remains fully visible.

At the landward end sits Harry's Café de Wheels, a legendary food cart operating since 1938, famous for its "Pie Floater": a meat pie floating upside down in a bowl of pea soup. A classic first meal for the adventurous.

Museum of Contemporary Art

Circular Quay West, The Rocks · 90 minutes

Australia’s premier museum dedicated to contemporary art is housed in a striking 1952 Art Deco building, formerly headquarters for the Maritime Services Board. Exhibiting art since 1991, its collection exceeds 4,000 works with a strong emphasis on Aboriginal and Torres Strait Islander artists.

Entry to the permanent collection is free, though major temporary exhibitions require tickets. A highlight is the top-floor cafe: its outdoor terrace offers panoramic views across the ferry wharves to the Opera House.

Ice Cream Break at the Harbour

Circular Quay, between ferry wharves and promenade · 45 minutes

The longest scheduled break of the day: After nearly three hours in the museum, sit by the seawall with an ice cream and watch the bustling harbour traffic. Iconic green-and-yellow ferries depart constantly for Manly, Taronga Zoo, and Watsons Bay.

In September, Sydney enters spring with mild temperatures around 20°C (68°F) – ideal for ice cream, though UV levels remain high, so sun protection is recommended.

Good to know
Local seagulls are notorious thieves. Keep a firm grip on your ice cream!

Sydney Opera House

Bennelong Point · 30 min exterior view, 1-hour guided tour

Danish architect Jørn Utzon won the international design competition in 1957. Construction took 16 years instead of the estimated four, finishing in 1973. The final cost reached $102 million AUD compared to the initial $7 million budget, largely funded through a state lottery.

Over 1,056,000 glazed Swedish ceramic tiles cover the iconic roof sails. Arranged in two shades of white, they reflect changing light from bright glare to warm cream tones. Designated a UNESCO World Heritage site in 2007, it hosts more than 1,800 performances annually.

Royal Botanic Garden

Located right behind the Opera House · 75 minutes

Established in 1816, this is Australia’s oldest scientific institution. Spanning 30 hectares, it showcases diverse flora from across the Pacific, populated by wandering Australian white ibises and native cockatoos.

The path along the eastern edge leads to Mrs Macquarie's Chair, a bench carved directly into the sandstone ridge in 1810. It provides the ultimate postcard view, capturing both the Opera House and Harbour Bridge in a single frame.

Bradleys Head / South Head · 60 minutes
